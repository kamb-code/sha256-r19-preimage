# Elimination search at the fourth constraint (R = 20), 2026-09-05/06

**Question.** Can any part of the 2^32 test at the fourth schedule constraint be
eliminated structurally, "from what we know"?  Seven independent explorers, one
angle each, CPU only, with two refuters queued for any lead.  No lead survived
(none was even claimed), so no refuters ran.

**Verdict.** EXCLUDED, gain 0 bits. No structural elimination of any part of the 2^32 at the fourth constraint exists from what we know. Seven independent angles (absorb a11; absorb a10 or any other context word; a second global/per-context table; re-assignment of swept/unknown/context words; partial evaluation and lookup reordering; a counting/information bound over all absorption schedules; and the wildcard list: reparametrising the digest, C2+C3 MITM, truncated tables, per-candidate v, structured a0, tying a4 to the swept word, neutral context words) all terminate at the same fact, derived symbolically (engines enumerating between 2,835 and 1.5 million legal context configurations per word) and confirmed numerically at full width with the real 16 GB table. The 2^45.4 decomposes as 2^32 (the C3 filter: one 32-bit equation on quantities already fully determined by a0 and the context) x 2^13.4 (the (3/4)^32 x 0.934 collapse loss, which is the cost of cutting the cycle a1->C1->a2->C2->a3->C3->a4->C0->a1 at its cheapest point and is not located at C3 at all). Every alternative frame that is legal costs 45.3-45.4 bits (translates and mirrors of the published frame; the best mirror was implemented with the real table and ties to three decimals: 0.2544 solutions per swept a0, collapse rate 1.0045e-4, C0 residual uniform) or 58.6 or more. No 1.5x-class lookup saving exists either: the collapsed condition needs a3, which needs the third lookup; the only sound pre-lookup test fires on 1.6e-6 of C2 lookups, and even skipping every C2 lookup would be 1.25x.

## What was excluded, with the blocking dependency named

The single blocking dependency, stated once and instantiated per word. Offset law: a context or unknown word a_k enters W_{k+5} through +Sigma0(a_k), carried by the h-term e_{k+1} = a_{k-3} + a_{k+1} - Sigma0(a_k) - Maj(a_k, a_{k-1}, a_{k-2}) subtracted inside W_{k+5}, and through Sigma1(e_{k+4}) with e_{k+4} = a_k + const. Sigma0 and Sigma1 are one-input rotate-XOR bijections; there is no second argument for a context word to saturate, and freezing the argument means fixing a_k. For k=4,5,6 the word W_{k+5} is the linear W_{9+j} term of C_{k-4}; for k>=6 it also sits under sigma1 in C_{k-6}. Second fact (forced homes): a1 can only be absorbed by C0 (it is heavy in C1, C2, C3 through Sigma0(a1) inside T2 of W2 and Sigma1(e1); measured 15.3/16.2/15.7 bits per flipped bit), a2 only by C1, a3 only by C2, in every one of the 11,086 legal configurations checked. Hence any fifth absorbed word must be mild (Maj/Ch only) in C0, C1, C2 and absorbable in C3, and for every k in 4..11 the offset law puts Sigma0(a_k) heavily into at least one of C0..C2 (minimum number of heavy constraints among C0,C1,C2 over 59,049-177,147 configurations per k: 1,1,2,2,2,3,3,2 for k=4..11, never 0). Per word: a4 -> C0 via Sigma0(a4) in e5 in W9 and Sigma1(e8), e8 = a4 + c8 (12.2-13.2 bits; the freeze residual Sigma0(a4)-Sigma0(v) vanishes only at a4 = v, verified exhaustively over 2^32 in 4 trials; the kernel Sigma0(a4)-Sigma1(a4+c8) is uniform to 24 bits even on the 2^16 admissible set Maj(a4,a3,a2)=a3). a5 -> C1 via Sigma0(a5) in e6 in W10 (12.1-12.3 bits; a5 is the one word that can be made deferrable in C0 and exactly linear in C3, but it dies at C1 in all 2,548 legal configurations). a6 -> C0 via sigma1(W14) with W14 = c - a6 through e10, and -> C2 via Sigma0(a6) in e7 in W11. a7 -> C0 via Sigma1(e8), e8 = c - Sigma0(a7) - Maj (15.7 bits), and -> C1 via sigma1(W15). a8, a9 -> C0 via W9/W14. a10 -> e14 = a10 + A14 - Sigma0(A13) - Maj(A13,A12,a11) (all other arguments digest words) -> Sigma1(e14) in W15 -> sigma1(W15) in C1, plus Ch(E16,e15,e14) in W17 (C1) and -e14 exactly linear in W18 (C2), and e10 in W14 under sigma1 (C0): heavy in all four constraints in all 1,092,281 legal configurations. a11 -> e15 = a11 + A15 - Sigma0(A14) - Maj(A14,A13,A12) -> Sigma1(e15) in W16 -> KC0 with coefficient +1 (and sigma1(W16) in KC2): 1280/1280 flips move KC0, KC1, KC2 and kappa3; two a11 values one bit apart share 0 of 2.13 million candidates. Also excluded: the digest fixing anything other than a12..a19 (bijective reparametrisation, 12 - 4 count invariant); MITM on the 64-bit (C2,C3) system (a4 is not free once a0 is swept; the C0 relation is a fresh random map of a4, distinct-a1 fraction 1 - 1/e); table geometry (C3 never touches the table); per-candidate v (v enters KC0 as Sigma0(v)-v plus a8(v), a9(v): 16.0 bits); structured a0 (the mirror frame, coverage 0.632 vs 0.634); tying a4 to a0 via phi(a0) (dimension count: replaces the 32-bit choice of v by a 32-bit tie; measured on 1.6e9 swept a0, P(c3=0 | candidate) = 2^-32, conditional halving z = +0.57; exhaustive in w-bit models at w = 8, 10, 12 with all c3 = 0 events verified as preimages); a neutral word moving kappa3 but not KC0..KC2 (none: every free word moves all three by 10.6-16.1 bits); multi-context amortisation (the candidate set is a function of (KC0,KC1,KC2,v) alone, invariance is a 96-bit fibre of a 128->96 map, m = 2 collision search costs 2^60.4 per preimage, worse for larger m, and even a free fibre only recovers the same 32 bits to a 2^13.4 floor); partial evaluation of c3 (every bit of c3 depends on every bit of a3, sensitivity min 0.089, GF(2) difference rank 32/32 and joint (c2,c3) rank 64/64, two roots of the same C2 key agree on c3 at 0.499 per bit); and using the collapse's ~16 forced a3 bits before the C2 lookup (carries in W3 = a3 + F23 destroy them; only a trailing forced run L >= 19 gives a sound test, 2^-19 x 2/3 of C2 lookups). Planting 2,000 genuine 20-round preimages confirmed the accounting exactly: the sweep returns the plant if and only if eps = 0 and the roots are stored (94/94 in the forced-eps arm).

## What remains open

Within the table-absorption paradigm (sweep one word, absorb by sigma0(u)-u / Gamma lookups, bitwise collapse, one residual test), nothing remains open at R=20: the enumerations are exhaustive over the menu of context conditions actually available to the family (equalities and complements among context and digest words, saturations of e8..e15 to 0 or -1 solved through a_r, a_{r-4} or a_{r-1}, ties to the swept word) and over all absorption orders and home assignments. What is genuinely untested is only what lies outside that menu, and none of it is promising: (1) context conditions that fix e_r to constants other than 0 and -1 or impose rotational relations between words; the prediction from the offset law is that they cannot help, because the blocking atoms are Sigma0(a_k) with a_k the unknown itself and Sigma1(a_k + c), and no constant choice removes a one-input function of the unknown. (2) The observed 1-bit GF(2) rank deficit of Sigma0 xor Sigma1 (rank 31): it produces a measurable excess of collisions of Sigma0(x) - Sigma1(x) in the XOR sense but the modular difference is uniform to 24 bits, so it does not transfer; even if it did, it would touch the freeze residual of a4 in C0, not C3. (3) Attacks that are not table absorption at all (differential, rebound, or W-domain sweeps with structured differences) are simply not addressed by this study; nothing here says they work, and the R=20 dependency structure gives no reason to expect them to. (4) The lookup-saving ceiling of 1.25x (skipping every C2 lookup) is a hard bound on constant-factor gains from scheduling, and the sound realisable fraction is ~3e-7 of lookups. The honest reading is that this line is closed and should be recorded as a theorem-level statement in the barrier paper: with forced homes a1->C0, a2->C1, a3->C2 and the offset law, no fifth word can be absorbed at R=20, and the cheapest cut of the resulting cycle is the family's Maj(v,a3,a2) = a3 at (3/4)^32.

## The closest miss

There is no surviving lead that removes bits. The closest miss, and the one thing worth writing down, is a5: it is the only word that can be made deferrable in C0 (a7 = a6, e8 = -1 gives C0 containing +a5 - Maj, 0.97 bits per flipped bit), mild in C2 (1.37 bits), and exactly linear in C3 (a7 = a6, e11 = 0; verified 200/200 at full width, independent of a0..a3 and of the rest of the context). It fails at exactly one edge, a5 -> C1 through Sigma0(a5) subtracted inside e6 = a2 + a6 - Sigma0(a5) - Maj(a5,a4,a3) in W10 (12.1 bits per flipped bit, present in all 2,548 legal configurations), the precise analogue of a4 -> C0. If the author wants one final falsification test rather than a repeat of what has been done, it is this: widen the context-condition menu beyond {equality, complement, e_r in {0,-1}} to arbitrary fixed values of e6..e11 and rotational ties between a4..a7, and run the existing 200-trial single-bit perturbation of a5 into C1 (scripts symdep_w.py / numcheck.py in elim/table2 and elim/a10 already accept new condition types). The prediction is that C1 stays heavy at ~12 bits in every case, because the blocking atom is Sigma0(a5) of the unknown itself with no context partner; a result below 2 bits would be the first crack in the barrier and would be worth a paper on its own. Everything else reported here (mirror frames, ties, reorderings) is at best an exact tie at 2^45.4 and should not be pursued further. Label for the record: no bit-level gain; no constant-factor gain above 1.0x found; the 1.25x ceiling on lookup scheduling is unreachable in practice.

## Plain English

The attack works by guessing one 32-bit word (a0) and then reading three unknown words off a precomputed table, one per equation; the fourth equation is a 32-bit check that either passes or fails, with no information available beforehand about which. The question was whether any structure could let us skip part of that check. Seven teams tried every route we could think of: absorbing a different word with the fourth equation, building a second table, changing which word is guessed and which are looked up, evaluating the check partially, merging it with the third equation, tying the context to the guess, and reusing one batch of candidates for several contexts. Every route runs into the same wall. In SHA-256 each word a_k is fed, five rounds later, into the message-recovery formula through Sigma0(a_k), a one-input scrambling function. With a two-input function you can sometimes fix one input to a constant that makes the other input irrelevant; with a one-input function there is nothing to fix, so the only way to make it harmless is to already know a_k. At 20 rounds there are twelve free words and four equations; three equations are already spoken for (a1 can only be recovered from the first, a2 from the second, a3 from the third), and whichever word the fourth equation would recover reaches one of the first three through such a one-input function. That closes the loop, and the cheapest way to break the loop is the one the paper already uses, which costs the 2^13.4 factor; the 2^32 is the fourth equation itself, and it cannot be shrunk because the quantities it tests are already completely determined by the guess. Two thousand planted solutions and a full run of an alternative arrangement with the real table match this accounting exactly. The honest conclusion is that this line is closed: the published 2^45.4 is the best any table-absorption frame can do at 20 rounds, and the only remaining untested ideas lie outside the family of tricks the attack is built from and have no structural reason to work.

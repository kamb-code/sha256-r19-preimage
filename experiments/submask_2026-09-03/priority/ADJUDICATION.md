## (1) The record, stated as a referee would accept it

The highest round count at which a preimage of the SHA-256 compression function has actually been **computed** and published is **19 rounds**: Oleg Zaikin, "Preimage attacks on round-reduced MD5, SHA-1 and SHA-256 using parameterized SAT solvers," *Constraints*, published online 23 January 2026, DOI 10.1007/s10601-025-09383-0 — the journal extension of Zaikin, "Inverting Step-Reduced SHA-1 and MD5 by Parameterized SAT Solvers," CP 2024, LIPIcs.CP.2024.31, whose abstract already states the result: the parameterized Cube-and-Conquer solver "for the first time inverted 29-step MD5, 24-round SHA-1, and 19-round SHA-256 compression functions." Model: standard IV, all 256 output bits fixed, all-ones digest, no padding constraint; cost 18 h 33 min on 192 CPU cores. Artifact: `github.com/olegzaikin/sha256sat`, directory `preimage_17-18-19r`. The ladder below it is 16 rounds (De/Kumarasubramanian/Venkatesan lineage and Homsirikamol et al., ~2012) and 17–18 rounds for the all-zero digest (Davydov, Pikhtovnikov, Kiryanova, Zaikin, *Sci. Tech. J. ITMO* 25(3) 2025, DOI 10.17586/2226-1494-2025-25-3-428-437), which also reports 19 rounds only in a weakened form **and, separately, 19-round collisions** — a fact your brief omitted and which should be stated so you are not accused of eliding their "19 rounds."

Everything published above 19 rounds is a complexity claim that has never been run. The current theoretical record is **47 steps at 2^255.6** (pseudo-preimage 2^253.2, memory 2^5), from Guo, Li, Liu, Wang, Zhang, "Dual-Syncopation Meet-in-the-Middle Attacks: New Results on SHA-2 and MD5," EUROCRYPT 2026, ePrint 2026/353, Springer 10.1007/978-3-032-25333-0_5 — **your related-work section is stale by two steps and one paper**: it also gives the first 46-step attack (2^254.5), improves 43/44/45 steps, extends SHA-512 to 51 steps (2^511.7), and improves full MD5 to 2^122.4. The prior record was 45 steps at 2^255.5 (Khovratovich–Rechberger–Savelieva, FSE 2012); 43 steps at 2^254.9 is Aoki–Guo–Matusiewicz–Sasaki–Wang, ASIACRYPT 2009; the cheapest figure anywhere in that entire line is 2^240 at 24 steps (Isobe–Shibutani, FSE 2009), i.e. the MITM literature has never come within astronomical distance of being executed at any step count.

## (2) Would 20 rounds be the first? Yes — with one computed artifact you must disclose

Yes. But the sharpest referee objection is not Zaikin; it is that **something has been computed at 44 steps**. The EUROCRYPT 2026 artifact (`github.com/desert-oasis/dual-sync-MITM`) contains `SHA256-44_partial_pseudo_preimage/`, which does output real messages: "Finds messages such that t = 32 or 40 output bits are set to zeros for 44-step SHA-256," at 2^28.02 and 2^35.38 loops (510 s and ~40 h, single-threaded), with the target formed as `A44 = Hash − A0` from a spliced internal state, not the standard IV. That is weakened on **three** of your six axes simultaneously — partial target (40 of 256 bits), free chaining value (pseudo-preimage), no padding — so it is not a preimage in your sense. The paper's other computation (`SHA256-44/`) is an 8-bit partial-matching statistical check over 2^17 samples that verifies the cost model and inverts nothing. **State both explicitly in your related work.** The largest number of SHA-256 output bits ever actually matched by a computed attack in the MITM line is 40 of 256, at 44 steps, with a free IV. Also pre-empt ePrint 2021/886 (Bouam, Bouillaguet, Delaplace, Noûs, "Controlling Half the Output of SHA-256"), which *is* a real witnessed computation on full 64-round SHA-256 but fixes only ~128 of 256 bits — a different point on the same two-dimensional trade-off, neither dominating the other.

One genuine priority risk that only one angle caught, and which I rate as the most likely SAT-community objection: the SAT-competition cryptographic benchmark generators (Nejati et al. 2017; Alamgir et al. 2024) emit CNFs labelled "preimage attacks on MD4, SHA-1 and SHA-256" with round count *d* ∈ [16,30] and "between 0 and 384 of the 512 input bits fixed." So nominally-20-to-30-round SHA-256 preimage instances exist, are distributed, and some are solved — and a solved SAT instance *is* a witness. They do not beat you (heavy message-bit fixing, targets derived from known messages so satisfiability is guaranteed, no round-count claim ever made from them), but you must say so in one sentence rather than let a referee say it.

## (3) Exact claim wording

Use this, or something within a word of it:

> We report the first preimage of the SHA-256 compression function reduced to 20 rounds that has actually been computed: standard IV, a single 512-bit block, feed-forward retained, no padding constraint, and all 256 output bits fixed to the target ⟨state the target explicitly⟩. The message block is published as a witness and can be verified in microseconds. The previous computed record was 19 rounds (Zaikin, *Constraints*, 2026), in the same model; Zaikin states that inverting the 20-round compression function would likely require additional effective algorithmic techniques. Published attacks at higher round counts are complexity claims that have never been executed — the best being 47 steps at 2^255.6 (Guo et al., EUROCRYPT 2026), improving the 45-step 2^255.5 biclique attack of Khovratovich et al. (FSE 2012) — and the cheapest such claim anywhere is 2^240 at 24 steps. To our knowledge, and to the best of our searches of the IACR ePrint archive, dblp, OpenAlex, arXiv and the SAT/CP/AI venues as of ⟨date⟩, no preimage of the SHA-256 compression function with all 256 output bits fixed has been exhibited beyond 19 rounds.

Required qualifications, each of which must appear somewhere and none of which may be dropped:

- **Computed vs theoretical.** Never write "first preimage attack on 20-round SHA-256" unqualified — the MITM line has covered 20 steps theoretically since 2009 and now reaches 47. Always pair the round count with "computed and verified" or "with a published witness."
- **Compression function vs hash.** Say "compression function," say the feed-forward is kept, and say the padding constraint is dropped — the same model as Zaikin, so the 19→20 comparison is apples-to-apples. Note in one line that this is *strictly stronger than a pseudo-preimage* at the same round count (IV fixed, not free) and *not directly comparable* to the MITM "Preimage T" column, which is the full hash and does pay for padding (their §5.1: "Padding consumes 65 DoF from W14, W15 and the MSB of W13").
- **Target choice — do not skip this.** State exactly which digest(s) you inverted and how they were chosen. If a target is H(known message), the instance is guaranteed satisfiable and drawn from the image of the round-reduced function; that is a materially weaker claim than inverting a structured digest not known a priori to have a preimage (Zaikin's all-ones, Davydov et al.'s all-zero). If you have a 20-round preimage for a structured or externally-specified target, lead with it; if not, say plainly that the targets are image-derived and that this matches ⟨whichever prior work it matches⟩. Given the project's history at R=19 (second preimage of Zaikin's all-ones digest), the strongest available move is to invert a target of the same kind at R=20 and say so.
- **Rounds vs steps.** One sentence: for SHA-256 both communities use "round" and "step" for the same 64 step-function applications (ePrint 2026/353's own table header says "Step" while its text says "rounds"); nobody groups 20 steps into a round as in SHA-1, so 19 rounds = 19 steps and no prior result becomes ≥20 under a different convention. Cheapest possible defence against a referee who assumes otherwise.
- **Knowledge cut-off.** Date the negative claim and name the indices searched, exactly as in the wording above. "To our knowledge, as of ⟨date⟩" — never a bare "first."
- **SHA-256 only.** The 51-step SHA-512 result must not be presented as comparable (different word size and step budget, still 2^509).

Two framings worth foregrounding, both defensible: the practical frontier reads 16 (2012) → 18 (2025) → 19 (Jan 2026) → 20 (yours), and yours is the first increment achieved on GPUs rather than a CPU SAT cluster; and 20/64 rounds computed versus 47/64 attacked at ~2^255 are two disjoint ladders — the EUROCRYPT 2026 bibliography cites *no* SAT-based preimage work at all (no Zaikin, no De et al., no Nossum, no Davydov), so the two literatures do not currently talk to each other. Their own sentence about "stagnation… leaving preimage attacks on SHA-2 and MD5 largely unchanged for more than a decade" is worth quoting.

## Where the four searches disagree or are thin — stated plainly

They do not disagree on the verdict; all four return 19 rounds and "nothing at 20+," from largely disjoint evidence bases (ePrint full-text, Zaikin's artifacts/venues, multilingual open web, and the EUROCRYPT artifact repo cloned and re-run). That convergence is the main reason I rate the negative claim high-confidence.

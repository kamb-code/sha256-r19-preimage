# Priority check for a computed 20-round SHA-256 preimage (2026-09-05)

Four independent searches (IACR ePrint and proceedings; the SAT/CP/AI venues
where the practical line actually lives; the open web including Russian and
Chinese sources; and the EUROCRYPT 2026 theoretical frontier), then an
adjudicator. Full reports in `reports.json`, the adjudication in
`ADJUDICATION.md`.

**Verdict: no preimage of the SHA-256 compression function has been computed and
published at 20 rounds or more — not full-target, not weakened, not free-start.
All four angles agree.**

## Corrections this forced to our own related work

* The theoretical record is **47 steps**, not 45: ePrint 2026/353 (Feb 2026)
  adds 46 and 47 steps, improves 43/44/45, takes SHA-512 to 51 steps and full
  MD5 to 2^122.4. Our papers cite only up to Khovratovich et al. 2012.
* **The sharpest objection to pre-empt is not Zaikin.** The EUROCRYPT 2026
  artefact (github.com/desert-oasis/dual-sync-MITM) computes real messages at
  **44 steps** setting 40 of 256 output bits to zero, from a spliced internal
  state (free chaining value). Weakened on three axes at once — partial target,
  pseudo-preimage, no padding — so not a preimage in our sense, but it is the
  largest number of SHA-256 output bits ever actually matched by a computed
  attack in the MITM line and must be stated explicitly.
* Also pre-empt ePrint 2021/886 (Bouam et al., "Controlling Half the Output of
  SHA-256"): a real witnessed computation on **full** SHA-256 fixing ~128 of 256
  bits. A different point on the same trade-off; neither dominates the other.
* SAT-competition benchmark generators (Nejati et al. 2017; Alamgir et al. 2024)
  emit CNFs labelled "SHA-256 preimage" with round counts 16–30, but fix up to
  384 of the 512 input bits and derive targets from known messages, so no round
  count has ever been claimed from them. Dispose of this in one sentence.
* Rounds and steps coincide for SHA-256 in both communities, so no prior result
  becomes >= 20 under a different convention. Say so once.
* The two literatures do not cite each other: ePrint 2026/353's bibliography
  contains no SAT-based preimage work at all. Their own sentence about
  "stagnation ... leaving preimage attacks on SHA-2 and MD5 largely unchanged
  for more than a decade" is worth quoting.

## Claim wording (use within a word of this)

> We report the first preimage of the SHA-256 compression function reduced to 20
> rounds that has actually been computed: standard IV, a single 512-bit block,
> feed-forward retained, no padding constraint, and all 256 output bits fixed to
> the target <state it>. The message block is published as a witness and can be
> verified in microseconds. The previous computed record was 19 rounds (Zaikin,
> Constraints, 2026), in the same model; Zaikin states that inverting the
> 20-round compression function would likely require additional effective
> algorithmic techniques. Published attacks at higher round counts are complexity
> claims that have never been executed — the best being 47 steps at 2^255.6 (Guo
> et al., EUROCRYPT 2026) — and the cheapest such claim anywhere is 2^240 at 24
> steps. To our knowledge, and to the best of our searches of the IACR ePrint
> archive, dblp, OpenAlex, arXiv and the SAT/CP/AI venues as of <date>, no
> preimage of the SHA-256 compression function with all 256 output bits fixed has
> been exhibited beyond 19 rounds.

Never write "first preimage attack on 20-round SHA-256" unqualified: the MITM
line has covered 20 steps theoretically since 2009. Always pair the round count
with "computed and verified" or "with a published witness", date the negative
claim, and name the indices searched.

## The qualification still outstanding

The 20-round preimage in `../R20_PREIMAGE.txt` is for a digest generated by the
solver, so the instance was known to be satisfiable. That is materially weaker
than inverting a digest not known in advance to have a preimage. Two pods are
attacking the all-ones digest — Zaikin's own 19-round target — at 20 rounds for
exactly this reason; if that lands it should lead the paper.
